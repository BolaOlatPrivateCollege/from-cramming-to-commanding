A familiar examination experience

“I read everything, but my brain just went blank.”

A few terms ago, a hardworking student sat across from my desk in the Principal's office, almost in tears.

She had studied late into the night, reading her notes repeatedly. Her parents had provided textbooks, paid for extra lessons and encouraged her to work hard.

But in the examination, a topic she had studied appeared in a different form—as a real-life scenario—and she struggled to answer it.

That experience stayed with me. Over the years, through teaching Mathematics and serving in school leadership, I have seen students work hard yet struggle to translate what they have read into good examination answers.

The problem is not always a lack of intelligence or effort. Sometimes, it is the way students have been taught to study.

Many students depend on memorising notes word for word. When an examiner changes the wording, introduces a scenario or asks them to explain something in their own words, they become confused.

What if we stopped telling students to simply read harder and started teaching them how to learn, think and answer questions?
