Why this book exists

She had studied. So why did her mind go blank?

A few terms ago, a hardworking student sat across from my desk in the Principal's office, almost in tears. She had studied late into the night, reading her notes repeatedly. Her parents had provided textbooks, paid for extra lessons and encouraged her to work hard.

But when the examination began, a topic she had studied appeared in a different form — as a real-life scenario — and she struggled to answer it. That experience stayed with me.

Over the years, through teaching Mathematics and serving in school leadership, I have seen many students work extremely hard yet struggle to translate what they have read into good examination answers. The problem is not always a lack of intelligence or effort. Sometimes, it is the way they have been taught to study.

The question that started it all

What if we stopped telling students to simply read harder — and started teaching them how to learn, think and answer questions?

Sound familiar?

Hard work isn't the problem.
The method is.

Many students have become dependent on memorising notes word for word. They can reproduce what they have read — but when an examiner changes the wording, introduces a scenario or asks them to explain something in their own words, they become confused.

03

No system for the exam hall

Without a clear way to break down a question and plan an answer, students freeze or write everything they remember.

If any of this sounds familiar, your child is not lazy — and not alone.

The answering framework

Meet D.E.C.O.D.E.

When a question looks unfamiliar, the problem is rarely the topic. It is the wording. D.E.C.O.D.E. gives students a repeatable way to slow down, break the question into parts and build an answer that responds to what the examiner actually asked.

D E C O D E

Before

Reads the question twice. Panics. Writes down everything remembered — even when it doesn't fit the question.

After

Decodes the question first. Identifies what is being asked. Answers in their own words, with a clear structure.

Same student. Same knowledge. A different method.
